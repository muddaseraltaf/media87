# Media87 SEO and Content Standard

Version: 1.0  
Created: 24 July 2026  
Status: project source of truth

## 1. Objective

Build a fast, credible, expandable website that helps the right organisation discover Media87, understand the relevant service or product, verify the team and evidence, and take a qualified next action.

Traffic, rankings, citations, engagement time, and page count are diagnostic signals. The primary outcomes are qualified enquiries, consultation bookings, attributable pipeline, product evaluation or adoption, and assisted conversions.

## 2. Strategic Classification

Media87 should initially be treated as:

- A professional service and digital marketing agency.
- A Dubai/UAE-relevant provider with a truthful local-service overlay.
- A product company for verified products such as ChatZen and LocalZen.
- An editorial publisher only where content supports audience decisions, brand authority, commercial pages, or original Media87 evidence.

This classification must be updated after the live site, business priorities, current products, target markets, and conversion data are verified.

## 3. Evidence Model

Label material claims and decisions:

- `observed`: directly visible on the live site, in code, in a first-party export, or in supplied evidence.
- `approved`: explicitly approved for publication by Media87.
- `external-primary`: current policy, documentation, research, law, or original dataset.
- `inference`: a reasoned conclusion that still requires confirmation.
- `hypothesis`: a proposed experiment with a success and failure condition.
- `unknown`: a fact or dataset that is not yet available.

Never turn an inference, public marketing statement, third-party estimate, or competitor claim into an approved fact.

## 4. Page Creation Gate

A new indexable URL must have all of the following:

1. A distinct user need or decision.
2. A business or product outcome.
3. One canonical owner for the intent.
4. Evidence that an existing page cannot own the need adequately.
5. A useful relationship to a parent hub or commercial page.
6. Original information, proof, process, tool, comparison, media, or judgement.
7. At least two plausible internal-link sources or a justified reusable navigation component.
8. A cannibalisation and duplication review.
9. An owner responsible for accuracy and maintenance.
10. An acceptance test and measurement plan.

If these are missing, choose one of:

- update an existing page;
- consolidate overlapping pages;
- keep the idea as an evidence request;
- produce a non-indexable campaign asset;
- record `no-new-page`.

Keyword wording alone is not a reason to create a page.

## 5. Commercial Page Quality

Use only the modules that help the visitor decide:

1. Clear page category, audience, relevant market, and primary action.
2. A short outcome statement with no unverified superlatives.
3. Who the service or product is for and when it is useful.
4. Problems, triggers, or use cases it addresses.
5. Scope, deliverables, inclusions, exclusions, requirements, or compatibility.
6. A real method or process with understandable steps.
7. Relevant proof close to the associated claim.
8. Differentiation grounded in capability, process, evidence, or product behaviour.
9. Constraints, risks, dependencies, and realistic expectations.
10. Pricing logic, starting range, or quote process when approved and maintainable.
11. The responsible team or person and relevant verified expertise.
12. Real sales or customer questions not already answered.
13. A clear next step, response expectation, and alternative contact path.

The first meaningful viewport should normally establish what the page offers, who or where it serves when relevant, why it matters, and what the primary action does.

## 6. Proof Standard

Prefer proof objects over generic claims:

- A case study with baseline, work completed, timeframe, result, attribution, caveat, and permission.
- An annotated campaign, deliverable, dashboard, website, creative, automation, or workflow.
- An original measurement, benchmark, dataset, survey, or calculation.
- A verified product demonstration or interface capture.
- A named expert explanation from a real qualified team member.
- A client testimonial with documented authenticity and publication permission.
- A real photograph or video of the team, client-approved work, process, event, or operating environment.
- Transparent limits, exclusions, and failure conditions.

Place proof beside the claim it supports. Do not rely on an unrelated logo wall or generic testimonial carousel.

## 7. Article Quality Standard

### 7.1 Before drafting

Complete the article brief and confirm:

- Audience and job to be done.
- Search and business intent.
- Current canonical owner and parent page.
- Search-result content type, format, and angle.
- Existing Media87 coverage and overlap.
- Primary and supporting questions.
- Original Media87 information gain.
- Evidence and source plan.
- Internal-link destinations and sources.
- Useful media or tool opportunity.
- Commercial next step.

### 7.2 Writing quality

- Open with the reader's problem, consequence, decision, or direct answer rather than a mechanical keyword sentence.
- Put the most important point early in each section.
- Every paragraph must add at least one of: explanation, mechanism, example, evidence, distinction, warning, constraint, decision criterion, or next action.
- Use plain, commercially literate language.
- Write as an experienced Media87 practitioner advising a client, without inventing delivery experience.
- Include non-obvious judgement or information gain that a generic summary could not provide.
- Use specific nouns and verbs rather than abstractions such as “unlock growth”, “revolutionise”, or “take your business to the next level”.
- Explain when an approach is useful, when it is not, and what it depends on.
- Treat AI as a capability, not a source of unsupported certainty or a substitute for business judgement.
- Use the approved Media87 English variant and terminology once the Brand Model is complete.
- Mention Dubai, UAE, GCC, an industry, or a neighbourhood only when the context is materially relevant and truthful.
- Do not use a fixed minimum or maximum word count. Use the shortest length that completely satisfies the task and evidence.
- Do not target keyword density. Use the language real customers and practitioners use.

### 7.3 Conditional formats

Add only when useful:

- A short answer for a direct question.
- Key facts or takeaways for a long or complex article.
- A table of contents when navigation materially helps.
- A table when readers must compare repeated attributes.
- A checklist when a real review or preparation process exists.
- Steps when order matters.
- FAQs sourced from Search Console, sales, support, reviews, on-site search, or research.
- A definition block when a term needs precision.

Do not add FAQ or HowTo content for rich-result markup. Its value must be visible to the reader.

### 7.4 Sources

- Research every article, even when visible citations are not required.
- Prefer current primary sources, official documentation, original research, recognised institutions, and first-party Media87 evidence.
- Use Media87 pages for Media87-specific claims, not as the only evidence for general industry claims.
- Put a source close to a factual claim when the format allows it.
- Verify dates, statistics, product features, platform policies, legal claims, and changing technical guidance immediately before publication.
- Summarise sources in original language; avoid unnecessary quotation.
- Remove or qualify any claim that cannot be verified.

### 7.5 Article trust

Where appropriate and true, include:

- Author name and role.
- Expert reviewer.
- Published and materially updated dates.
- Editorial or testing methodology.
- Disclosure of AI assistance when context warrants it.
- Corrections mechanism.

Never fabricate an author, reviewer, delivery anecdote, test, date, or first-hand experience.

## 8. Location Page Gate

A location page may be proposed only when:

1. Media87 genuinely serves the location or has a real staffed location there.
2. The page answers a materially distinct local decision.
3. Service availability, language, currency, contact path, and operating model are accurate.
4. At least three categories of maintainable local value are available, such as:
   - a local client or case study with permission;
   - original local photography or video;
   - actual service constraints, logistics, availability, or response process;
   - neighbourhoods or districts genuinely served;
   - local platform, audience, regulatory, language, or industry considerations;
   - a local team member or partner;
   - real location-specific questions;
   - locally relevant pricing logic or deliverables.
5. The page has distinct internal links and will not merely funnel to an identical generic page.
6. A maintenance owner can keep the information current.

Do not:

- produce city-name swaps;
- fabricate an office, address, phone number, local team, result, review, photograph, or service area;
- create every service × location combination automatically;
- add LocalBusiness markup for a location that is not a qualifying real business location;
- use hidden or repetitive local text to inflate relevance.

For a service-area page without a physical branch, make the relationship explicit and use organisation/service markup that reflects reality.

## 9. Internal Linking and Ownership

Every important page should receive crawlable links from relevant pages.

For each proposed link record:

- Source URL or reusable component.
- Destination URL.
- Natural anchor and surrounding context.
- Placement.
- User purpose.
- Acceptance test.

Prefer:

- Hub to service/product/location/article.
- Service/product back to its hub.
- Article to the most relevant commercial owner.
- Commercial page to relevant proof, team, method, and case study.
- Case study back to the service, product, industry, and contact path.
- Related content based on a real next question, not automated tag similarity alone.

Avoid generic “learn more” anchors when the destination can be described naturally. Avoid repetitive exact-match anchors.

## 10. Metadata and Search Appearance

Each canonical indexable page needs:

- A unique, accurate title describing the page.
- A page-specific meta description when a useful summary can be written.
- A stable, readable URL.
- A self-referential canonical.
- Open Graph and social preview fields aligned with visible content.
- An accurate representative image where appropriate.
- Breadcrumbs for hierarchical pages.
- Intentional robots and preview controls.

There is no universal title or meta-description character limit. Preview likely truncation, but optimise for clarity and accuracy.

Keep the title, visible primary heading, breadcrumb, canonical owner, Open Graph fields, internal anchors, and structured data aligned around the same page entity.

## 11. Structured Data

- Use JSON-LD where practical.
- Maintain one stable organisation identity and reuse its `@id`.
- Describe only visible, truthful, current content.
- Use the most specific appropriate type without chasing unsupported rich-result features.
- Connect WebSite, WebPage, BreadcrumbList, Organization, Person, Service, Product, SoftwareApplication, Article, Place, or other verified entities coherently.
- Use LocalBusiness only for a qualifying real business location.
- Do not mark a service as a product merely to seek review stars.
- Do not mark up self-serving ratings where ineligible.
- Validate Schema.org syntax, current search-feature eligibility, rendered output, and visible-content agreement separately.

Schema improves machine clarity and supported feature eligibility. It is not an AI-citation or ranking shortcut.

## 12. Media Standard

Every media item needs a job:

- Identify a real person, place, service, product, or deliverable.
- Demonstrate a process, interface, result, or comparison.
- Provide local or first-hand evidence.
- Explain a system or decision.
- Supply a representative search/social preview.
- Decorate without displacing the core task.

For each page choose `keep`, `add`, `replace`, `remove`, or `omit`.

Prefer original photography, annotated work, product demonstrations, diagrams, charts, and rights-cleared client evidence. Do not use stock or AI imagery that implies a false client, office, team, event, or result.

Technical requirements:

- Responsive `img` or `picture` output with a fallback.
- Appropriate AVIF/WebP delivery.
- Width and height or an explicit aspect ratio.
- Useful contextual alt text, or empty alt text for decorative redundancy.
- Captions when evidence needs context.
- No lazy loading on the likely LCP image.
- Lazy loading for suitable below-fold assets.
- Crawlable, stable image URLs.

## 13. Technical and Accessibility Baseline

- HTTPS and one canonical host.
- Correct status codes and one-hop redirects.
- Valid robots rules and XML sitemaps containing canonical indexable URLs.
- Custom 404 and intentional 404/410 behaviour.
- Static or server-rendered important content.
- Crawlable `<a href>` links.
- Logical semantic landmarks and heading structure.
- Accessible labels, focus states, contrast, keyboard behaviour, and dynamic-state announcements.
- Mobile layout without horizontal overflow or blocked zoom.
- Working menus, forms, product journeys, contact methods, and success/error states.
- No duplicated canonical, robots, metadata, schema IDs, form IDs, or analytics events.
- No production dependency on staging assets.

Performance targets at the 75th percentile:

- LCP ≤ 2.5 seconds.
- INP ≤ 200 milliseconds.
- CLS ≤ 0.1.

A lab score is diagnostic; field evidence is the outcome.

## 14. Review and Reputation Integrity

- Request honest reviews from eligible customers without incentives or pressure.
- Do not discourage negative reviews or selectively request positive reviews.
- Do not ask customers to insert locations, services, staff names, or target keywords.
- Respond professionally to positive and negative reviews.
- Use only authentic testimonials with permission.
- Report policy-violating reviews through the platform process; do not promise removal of legitimate criticism.

Media87 product and service copy must not imply review gating.

## 15. Migration Protection

Before launch:

1. Inventory all current URLs, statuses, metadata, canonicals, media, schema, links, and conversion events.
2. Import Search Console, analytics, CRM, backlink, and paid-campaign landing-page evidence when available.
3. Classify each URL: preserve, update, consolidate, redirect, retire, or unknown.
4. Preserve useful URL paths whenever practical.
5. Create a one-to-one redirect map for justified changes.
6. Update internal links, canonical tags, hreflang, sitemaps, structured data, analytics, ads, and profiles.
7. Test staging without allowing accidental indexation.
8. Remove temporary `noindex` controls from approved production pages.
9. Validate status, redirect, canonical, rendering, structured data, forms, and analytics before cutover.
10. Submit the sitemap and annotate the launch.
11. Monitor protected pages, queries, conversions, errors, and redirects.
12. Keep permanent redirects for at least one year and preferably indefinitely when useful.

Do not combine unnecessary domain, URL, design, content, and measurement changes if a phased migration can reduce risk.

## 16. Automation Workflow

Automation may:

- Inventory pages and assets.
- Pull approved first-party data.
- Group queries and user needs.
- Identify overlap and missing evidence.
- Create structured briefs and draft metadata.
- Scaffold page files from approved records.
- Generate drafts and media briefs.
- Validate required fields, links, metadata, schema, images, performance budgets, and build output.
- Create preview deployments.
- Produce refresh and consolidation recommendations.

Automation must not:

- manufacture evidence;
- choose a location-page footprint from keyword lists alone;
- publish unreviewed claims;
- create fake variation to evade duplication;
- change high-value URLs without migration approval;
- submit forms, alter profiles, contact clients, or publish externally without explicit authority.

Required status values:

`proposed → researched → brief-approved → draft → fact-reviewed → seo-qa → design-qa → approved → published → measured → refresh-or-consolidate`

## 17. Acceptance Tests for Every Published Page

- Page has a distinct role, user task, canonical owner, and business outcome.
- Primary content and links exist in rendered HTML.
- Visible content, title, canonical, breadcrumb, social fields, and structured data agree.
- Claims and proof are approved and traceable.
- Desktop and 390 px mobile layouts are usable.
- No horizontal overflow, clipped text, hidden critical content, or obstructed primary action.
- Keyboard, focus, labels, errors, and success states work.
- Internal links use the correct canonical destinations.
- Images have the correct job, rights, dimensions, responsive sources, and alt treatment.
- The page is in the appropriate sitemap and is not accidentally blocked.
- No console error, broken request, staging dependency, or duplicated analytics event affects the core journey.
- Baseline, publication annotation, outcome metric, guardrail, owner, and review date are recorded.

## 18. Explicitly Rejected Legacy Rules

Do not import these older checks as automatic requirements:

- Primary keyword density of one to two percent.
- A requirement to place the city in every H1, title, or image alt.
- A requirement for exactly three to five geo entities.
- A fixed number of service pages.
- A universal three-click rule as a substitute for contextual architecture.
- Exact title length of 45–60 characters.
- Exact meta-description length of 140–160 characters.
- Exactly one H1 as a ranking rule rather than a clarity/accessibility review.
- HTML sitemap as a default requirement.
- LocalBusiness markup on every homepage.
- `hasMap` on pages where no appropriate real place exists.
- Reviewer keyword coaching, Local Guide targeting, review-photo quotas, or seeded Google Q&A.
- A fixed posting cadence, image count, FAQ count, article word count, or schema graph size.

These may become site-specific tests only when supported by a real user need, platform policy, observed problem, or measurable hypothesis.
